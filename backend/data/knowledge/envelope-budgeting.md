Daybook uses envelope budgeting. Each month, income is divided into named categories (envelopes) -- like "Groceries", "Rent", or "Fun money". Every expense is charged against one category, and that category's remaining balance is what's actually available to spend on it for the rest of the month.

A category's "allocated" amount is what was planned for it this month, plus any "rollover" carried in from last month. Its "spent" amount is the sum of expenses (minus any refunds) charged to it. "Available" is allocated minus spent -- if available goes negative, the category is overspent.

A category can be marked "rollover-enabled". When a month closes, a rollover-enabled category's leftover available balance (positive or negative) carries forward as next month's starting rollover, instead of resetting to zero. This rewards saving inside an envelope and carries overspending forward as a reminder rather than erasing it.

"Unallocated" is income minus everything planned across all categories -- money not yet assigned anywhere. A healthy month usually assigns all of it somewhere before spending begins.

Wallets (cash, bank, mobile money) are separate from categories -- a wallet tracks where money physically sits; a category tracks what it's earmarked for. The same expense has both a wallet (where the money came from) and a category (what it was for).
