I built my own personal AI. And yes, it reminds me about things my wife asked me to do. 🙂

Meet DayBook AI — my own small step toward the JARVIS/FRIDAY idea, built for everyday life.

It brings together the context I choose to share: my work, plans, notes, routines, and the little commitments that are easy to forget.

It helps me manage tasks and my calendar, sends morning briefings, and follows up on unfinished work. I can speak to it, return to earlier conversations, and use it across my phone and computer.

The part that matters most to me: I built the assistant, and its open-weight AI model runs on my own server.

I wanted useful personal AI with more control over where my conversations are processed, how memory works, and how my information is managed. Self-hosting gives me more control — and more responsibility. Calendar and notification integrations still have their own data flows.

My work across public health, data systems, and AI has shaped how I approach this: context matters, trust matters, and a system needs to do useful work beyond producing a convincing answer.

I also want an assistant that helps me think through difficult situations and challenges my assumptions, instead of automatically agreeing with me.

DayBook AI is still evolving. But it is already part of my daily life.

Built by me. Shaped around my life. Designed to help me follow through.

If you had your own personal AI, what would you want it to remember — and what would you keep off-limits?
